Pacifica–Cascadia Secession Charter

A Framework for Lawful Separation, Constitutional Reconstitution, and Sovereign Union

---

I. Foundational Principle: The Right to Resume Liberty

We, the People of the Pacifica–Cascadia region, affirm that all legitimate political authority derives from the consent of the governed, and that such consent is neither perpetual nor irrevocable.

When governing institutions exceed the rightful limits of their delegated authority, infringe upon unalienable rights, or operate in sustained contradiction to natural justice, they enter into a condition properly understood as a state of political war against the People. In such a condition, the People retain both the right and the duty to resume their original liberty and to institute new governance grounded in the preservation of their rights.

This Charter constitutes a lawful, peaceful, and structured mechanism for that restoration.

-

Equal Sovereignty Clause

No distinction in dignity, rights, or accession authority shall exist between Member Entities originating from differing national jurisdictions. All derive legitimacy equally from the consent of their People.

-

III. Structural Doctrine: Bottom-Up Sovereignty

Political legitimacy originates at the local level.
Local divisions (counties, regional districts, municipios) are the fundamental unit of consent
States, provinces, and territories serve as coordinating authorities
The Federation exists only through the aggregation of freely consenting communities

No entity shall be incorporated by force, coercion, or administrative decree.

-

IV. Unified Mechanism: Amendment and Referendum Integration

1. Constitutional or Legislative Authorization

Each Member Entity must adopt a Sovereignty Initiative Amendment or equivalent legal instrument which:
Recognizes the right of withdrawal from external governance
Mandates facilitation of secession processes
Authorizes constitutional conventions and negotiations
Commits to peaceful and lawful transition

2. Localized Referendum Process

A petition of no less than 5% of eligible voters triggers a referendum
All local divisions vote independently
Minimum turnout threshold: 10%
Each division casts one unit vote

3. Accession Threshold

A minimum of three-quarters (75%) of all local divisions must vote in the affirmative
If achieved, the entire Member Entity accedes
If not achieved, the process resets

-

V. Sequenced Accession Process

Phase 1 – Legal Activation

Passage of sovereignty authorization
Public education and civic engagement

Phase 2 – Petition and Trigger

Petition threshold met
Referendum scheduled

Phase 3 – Local Decision

Transparent and audited voting process

Phase 4 – Constitutional Convention

Delegates convene across all participating regions
Drafting and refinement of the Pacifica Constitution

Phase 5 – Accession and Transition

Certification of results
Entry into the Federation
Negotiation with prior national governments

-

VI. Rights Preservation and Legal Continuity

Existing laws remain in force unless they violate natural rights or the Pacifica Constitution

Indigenous sovereignty and treaty obligations remain fully protected

Civil and individual rights remain intact throughout transition

Participation remains voluntary and free from coercion

-

VII. Statement of Justification

This Charter arises from sustained and systemic grievances, including:
Erosion of constitutional protections
Expansion of centralized authority beyond lawful limits
Failure of representative accountability
Economic systems misaligned with individual sovereignty
Manipulation of public information and perception

When such conditions persist over time, they constitute a pattern of usurpation. Under such circumstances, the People retain the right to withdraw consent and establish new governance structures aligned with natural law.

-

VIII. International Transition Framework

To ensure legitimacy and stability, the following principles govern external relations:

1. Good Faith Negotiation

Peaceful negotiation with the United States, Canada, and Mexico
Respect for existing treaties where consistent with natural rights

2. Recognition Strategy

Pursuit of international recognition through established legal norms
Adherence to principles of self-determination

3. Economic Continuity

Maintenance of trade relationships during transition
Gradual assumption of monetary and fiscal sovereignty

4. Non-Aggression Principle

Absolute renunciation of offensive force
Defensive posture only

IX. Legal Compatibility and Constraints

United States

Constitutional tensions with federal supremacy
Reliance on political negotiation and consent-based legitimacy

Canada

Requirement for provincial consent and federal negotiation
Potential Supreme Court involvement

Mexico

Federal constitutional limitations on secession
Necessity of legal reform or negotiated autonomy pathway

-

X. Nature of the Pacifica Federation

Pacifica shall be:
A voluntary federation
Grounded in natural rights
Structurally decentralized

Governed through transparent and participatory systems

It shall not replicate centralized or coercive systems of governance.

-

XI. Final Declaration

We declare that:

We are no longer morally bound to systems that violate their foundational principles

We will pursue separation through lawful and peaceful means

We will establish governance grounded in liberty, responsibility, and justice

This action is undertaken not in defiance of rightful authority, but in defense of it.
